    The rules we simulate

    Assumptions behind the solver
    • One player against the banker, 1-unit bets.
    • A fresh 52-card deck every hand, so card-removal odds are exact.
    • Both sides play optimally. The banker sees your card count and plays his best reply; you play yours. Solved as a two-player game with CFR+, to within 0.002% per hand.
    • The five-card bust penalty is for players only. A banker who busts on five cards pays 1×.
    • Your strategy uses only your own cards.
    Not modelled
    • Several players at one table. A real banker can reveal some players at 16 or 17, then keep drawing against the rest. The solved banker captures the core of that: playing differently by how many cards you hold.
    • Playing several hands from one deck without reshuffling (card counting).

    Why the strategy works

    The whole chart fits in one line and five short rules. Each comes with the reason, so you can rebuild it at the table without memorising a grid.

    The one line to remember

    Stand from 16 · 17 · 19 · 20. Hit everything below.

    16 Two-card hard hand
    17 Hard hand of three or four cards: no Ace, or the Ace counts 1
    19 Two cards with an Ace counted 11
    20 Three cards with an Ace counted 10

    This is the complete chart for the 2× five-card bust rule, with both sides playing optimally. The banker still keeps about 3.1% per hand. Players who copy him and stand on every 16 lose 5.4%. With the 1× rule, also hit four-card 17 and three-card Ace-high 20.

    The question everyone asks

    “Hitting 16 to 19? Surely the bust risk is huge.”

    We never hit a hard 17, 18 or 19. Like a casino player, you stand on those. The “hit 16 to 19” rule is only for three cards with an Ace counted as 10, and there the next card cannot bust you.

    Hard 17

    68% chance the next card busts you. Stand

    Three cards, Ace counted 10, total 19

    0% chance the next card busts you. Hit

    So why not stand on 19?

    Because the hit is free and the hand gets a shot at 五龙. After the hit, with the 2× rule:

    • Four-card 20 or 21 32.5%
    • Four-card 17 to 19 23.8%
    • 五龙, paid 2× or 3× 25.1%
    • Five-card bust 18.7%

    The only bust risk comes later, if you land on 11 to 16 and need a fifth card. Even then the fifth card makes 五龙 more often than it busts.

    Rule 2 · hard hands

    Hard 16: stand on two cards, hit on three or four. Stand on hard 17 or more.

    Why stand on two-card 16? It hides. A banker who sees two cards standing has to assume anything from 16 to 20, so he never stops on 16 against you and busts about 35% of the time trying to beat you. That makes standing exactly as good as hitting (both about −0.34). The solver hits it one time in four, at random, so the banker can't read you, but that is worth less than 0.1% per hand. Just stand.

    Why hit 16 on three or four cards? Once you've drawn, the banker expects 17 or better. A standing 16 can only win when he busts. Here is how the banker finishes against a three-card stand:

    • Banker busts 31.2%: your 16 wins
    • Banker stops on 16 1.1%: push
    • Banker makes 17 or better 67.7%: your 16 loses

    A standing 16 loses two hands in three. Hitting gives you about a 40% shot at 17 to 21, and when you bust, a banker bust still turns it into a push.

    Why stop at 17? About 7 cards in 10 would bust it.

    Why a 16 on two cards isn't a 16 on four

    The chart gives the same total a different EV depending on how many cards you hold. Two things cause it: the banker reads your card count, and your own cards change what's left in the deck. Exact numbers for the rule chosen at the top:

    1 · The banker reads your card count

    Look at the stand side. Against two cards the banker busts – and never stops on 16, because your hand could be a 20. Against four cards he busts only – and stops on 16 – of the time, because half of four-card players have already busted.

    His 16 only ties your 16 but loses to your 17. So more cards make a standing 16 worse and a standing 17 better.

    2 · Card removal

    Look at the hit side. Only an Ace to 5 saves a 16. A two-card 16 like 10-6 leaves all 20 of them in the deck. A three-card 16 like 2-4-10 has used two of them up: on average – are left, and – for four cards.

    Close call: two-card hard 16

    Standing wins only when the banker busts. Hitting wins more often but busts you 60% of the time, and a third of those busts are saved by a banker bust. The two sums land almost on top of each other.

    Four-card hard 17: the bust rule decides it

    Hit, and about 28% of cards (Ace to 4) make 五龙, paid 2× or 3×. Everything else busts you. With a 1× bust the wins outweigh the losses; with a 2× bust the same busts cost double and standing wins.

    What the banker knows

    Your cards are face down, but the banker can count them.

    Two cards, standingYou hold 16 to 20.
    Three cardsYou drew once: usually 17 to 21, or a silent bust.
    Five cards五龙 or a five-card bust. Nothing in between.

    Where the banker's edge comes from

    The rules are nearly symmetric, and two busts push, so the casino's usual edge (you bust first and lose anyway) is gone. The banker wins anyway, about 3.1% per hand, with information: he plays last and can count your cards, while you see nothing of his.

    Opening people early is how he cashes it in. He settles the players he has nothing to gain from drawing against, like five-card hands, then keeps drawing against the rest. A bust after that only costs him against the players still waiting.

    The player-only 2× five-card bust adds about 2% more for him. Take the bank when it comes round.

    When you hold the bank

    • Player stood on two cards: with your own two-card 17, hit about 70% of the time. Their stand usually means 17 to 20.
    • Your four-card 16 or 17: hit against players holding two to four cards. Your five-card bust only costs 1×.
    • Five-card players (2× rule): stand on any hard 16 or more. They hold 五龙, which beats you anyway, or a bust that pays you 2× unless you bust too.
    • Ace counted high: hit up to 18 on two cards and up to 19 on three, because the next card can't bust you.

    How these numbers were made

    Exact solve. All 2,983 possible hands of two to five cards from one deck were enumerated with exact card-removal odds. Your best play depends on how the banker plays and his depends on yours, so it is a two-player game, solved with CFR+ until neither side could gain more than 0.002% per hand by changing strategy. That is the same idea as solving poker: both sides are assumed to play perfectly.

    Check it yourself. Tap any cell on the Strategy chart tab. Your browser recomputes that cell from scratch with a separate program that walks every next card and every way the banker can play, shows the solver's numbers next to its own, and can deal the hand 200,000 times as a third check. The hit side is plain arithmetic: each next card's chance times what the new hand is worth.

    Monte Carlo check. 20 million shuffled hands per matchup landed within about two standard errors of the exact values every time. The bust chances and outcome splits on this page come from 3 to 6 million simulated hands each. Run your own on the Play tab.

    Why it's fast. Poker and liar's dice need CFR over millions or billions of situations. Banluck is the same kind of game, but the banker only ever sees your card count, so it is small enough to solve in about two and a half minutes.
