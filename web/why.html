The question everyone asks

“Hitting 16 to 19? Surely the bust risk is huge.”

We never hit a hard 17, 18 or 19. Like a casino player, you stand on those. The “hit 16 to 19” rule is only for three cards with an Ace counted as 10, and there the next card cannot bust you.

The five rules

Rule 1 · forced

Under 16, you hit. No choice.

The table won't let you stand below 16. Even 7-7 draws, and the third 7 pays 7×.

Rule 2 · hard hands

Hit hard 16. Stand on hard 17 or more.

Why hit 16 at 60% bust? The banker must also reach 16, so a standing 16 can only win when the banker busts. Here is how a fixed banker finishes:

  • Banker busts 21.0%: your 16 wins
  • Banker stops on 16 14.4%: push
  • Banker makes 17 or better 64.6%: your 16 loses

A standing 16 loses two hands in three. Hitting gives you a 40% shot at 17 to 21, and when you bust, the banker's 21% bust chance still turns it into a push.

Why stop at 17? A 17 already beats every banker who stops on 16, and 7 cards in 10 would bust it.

Rule 3 · two cards with an Ace

A-5, A-6, A-7: hit. A-8, A-9: stand.

Why hit A-7 (18)? The third card can't bust you. The Ace drops from 11 to 10 or 1, and the lower value always keeps you at 21 or under.

Why stand on A-8 (19)? 19 is already strong. A draw from 19 makes you worse more often than better.

Against a smart banker

Your cards are face down, but the banker can count them.

Change one thing

Stand on a two-card hard 16 most of the time. The solver hits it about one time in four, at random. If you always hit 16, every two-card stand would show 17 or more, and the banker could hit 17 against you safely.

With best play you lose about −3.1% per hand against a smart banker, against +2.6% against a fixed one. The banker's information decides the game, so take the bank when it comes round.

When you hold the bank

  • Player stood on two cards: with your own two-card 17, hit about 70% of the time. Their stand usually means 17 to 20.
  • Your four-card 16 or 17: hit against players holding two to four cards. Your five-card bust only costs 1×.
  • Five-card players (2× rule): stand on any hard 16 or more. They hold 五龙, which beats you anyway, or a bust that pays you 2× unless you bust too.
  • Ace counted high: hit up to 18 on two cards and up to 19 on three, because the next card can't bust you.

How these numbers were made

Exact solve. All 2,983 possible hands of two to five cards from one deck were enumerated with exact card-removal odds. Against the fixed banker, the best play for every hand comes from working backwards from five cards. Against the smart banker it is a two-player game, solved with CFR+ until neither side could gain more than 0.002% per hand.

Monte Carlo check. 20 million shuffled hands per matchup landed within about two standard errors of the exact values every time. The bust chances and outcome splits on this page come from 3 to 6 million simulated hands each. Run your own on the Play tab.

Why it's fast. Against a fixed banker this is a one-player problem, solved in under a second. Poker and liar's dice need CFR over millions or billions of situations because both sides hide information. The smart-banker version of Banluck is that kind of game too, but small enough to solve in about two and a half minutes.
