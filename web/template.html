Basic strategy

The dealer's cards are face down, so there is no upcard column. The decision depends on just three things you can see: your total, how many cards you hold, and whether an Ace is counted high. Under 16 you must hit; at five cards you stop.

The rules we simulate

Banluck is the blackjack Singaporean and Malaysian families play over Chinese New Year. House rules vary from table to table; this is the exact set the solver and simulator use, next to standard casino blackjack.

Payout ladder

Higher beats lower. A hand that wins is paid at its own rung; a hand that loses pays the winner's rung. Same rung is a push, except regular totals, which compare.

3×Ban-Ban: two Aces on the deal. Settled immediately; beats Ban-Luck.
2×Ban-Luck: Ace + 10, J, Q or K on the deal. Settled immediately.
7×777: exactly three 7s. Best finished hand.
3×Five-card 21: five cards totalling exactly 21.
2×Five-card Charlie: five cards, 21 or less. Beats any regular total.
1×Regular hand: 16 to 21; higher total wins, ties push.
−1×Bust: over 21. Push if the banker also busts.
−2×Five-card bust (2× variant): over 21 on your fifth card. Push if the banker also busts.

Order of play (heads-up)

  1. Two cards each, all face down.
  2. Any Ban-Ban or Ban-Luck is shown and settled at once. If either side had one, the hand is over.
  3. You hit until you reach at least 16, then hit or stand as you like, up to five cards. If you bust you keep quiet; your cards stay down.
  4. The banker plays, knowing only how many cards you hold. The banker must also reach 16, up to five cards.
  5. Both hands are revealed and settled on the ladder.

Parameters fixed for the solver

  • One player against the banker; 1-unit bets.
  • Fresh 52-card deck each hand, so card removal is exact.
  • Fixed dealer: hits below 16, stands on any 16+, no matter what you hold.
  • Smart dealer: sees your card count and picks the play that hurts you most. Solved as a two-player game with CFR+, until neither side can gain more than 0.002% per hand.
  • The five-card bust penalty applies to the player only. A banker who busts on five cards pays the normal amount.
  • Your strategy uses only your own cards; it never assumes you know what the banker holds.

Not modelled

  • Several players at one table. In a real game the banker can reveal some players at 16 or 17, then keep drawing against the rest. The smart dealer here captures the core of that edge: playing differently depending on how many cards you hold.
  • Running a deck through several hands without reshuffling (card counting).
